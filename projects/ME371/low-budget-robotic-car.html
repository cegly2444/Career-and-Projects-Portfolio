Low-Budget Gear-Shifting Robotic Car

Skills Used

Mechanical Design Arduino Mechanical Linkages Low-Budget Design Motor Control

Project Overview

  • Course & Context: Designed as a functional gear switching robotic car for ME 371 (Mechanical Design II).
  • My Role: Designed the gear shifting mechanism, assisted with designing the gear train, wrote the Arduino script that allowed the gearing to be set via remote, and made component mounts for the chassis.
  • Objectives: Using a low-power motor, design a robotic car with a transmission optimized for seven different categories/events.

CAD & Mechanical Linkages

The heart of this project was the transmission. The car needed to be fast, strong, and agile, though not all at the same time. This meant we needed to design a system that could change to highspeed gears with low torque, slow gears with high torque, a reverse gear with medium speed and torque, and it needed to be able to swap gears quickly to excel in the agility category while avoiding jamming.

The most difficult aspect of the transmission design was figuring out how to shift between 5 different gears with only 2 servos that had limited power and rotation range. I settled on using a dog clutch mechanism to engage the gears, with one servo having 3 positions (idle, gear 1, and gear 2), and the other servo having 4 positions (idle, gear 3, gear 4, and reverse). The shifter was designed with a relatively simple sliding mechanical linkage that converted the rotation of the servos to linear translation using a pin slider and proper constraints. For each gear, I recorded the angles of both servos required for that gear, then programmed them into an Arduino script that would command the servos to rotate to those angles.

Dog Clutch Shifter sliding to engage different gears

Outcome

The car performed very well, getting perfect marks on 6 of the 7 categories, and getting 7.5/8 on the last category. This resulted in our team placing 1st in the class, which we were very proud of. I also learned a lot during this project, especially about minimizing inefficiencies and friction and reducing the complexity of systems to lower the potential causes of failure.
